        How Inventory works
        1. + Add Product: create an item once, with its prices and starting stock.
        2. Edit (on the product's row): change its prices or alert number. The stock number can't be typed over. Change it with one of the 3 stock buttons inside Edit, so every change is recorded:
        3. + Stock in: a delivery arrived from your supplier. Stock goes up and the cost is saved as a purchase.
        4. − Damaged / lost: items you can't sell. Stock goes down and the cost is counted as a loss.
        5. Correct count: your shelf count doesn't match the system. Enter the real count and why. It's not counted as a purchase or a loss.
        6. Sales at Checkout lower the stock automatically. Each product has its own low-stock alert number (5 by default); at or below it, the product is marked Low.
        7. Click a summary card (In stock, Low stock…) to show only those products.

        How does the system decide what is unusual?
        • Compared with normal: each staff member is compared with the rest of the team over the last 30 days, and each product with its own past 90 days. Only big differences are flagged, so normal ups and downs are ignored.
        • Returns and cancellations: a seller whose sales are returned or cancelled much more often than everyone else's (anomaly detection by rate comparison).
        • Quick voids: sales cancelled or returned within 15 minutes, a common sign of "ring up, take the cash, then cancel".
        • Discounts: a seller who gives discounts far more often than others, or whose discounts keep landing right at the staff limit.
        • Missing stock: repeated "Correct count" changes that lower the same product (shrinkage).
        • Damaged / lost spikes: a product written off much more than usual.
        • Odd hours and outliers: sales at hours the shop almost never sells, and sale amounts far bigger than normal (statistical z-score).
        • Severity: High = very far from normal or repeated; Medium = clearly unusual; Low = worth a quick look.

        Sales Forecast & Restock Planner

        Uses your completed sales history to estimate upcoming product demand and stockout timing.

        How it works: it looks at what sold in the last 60 days (recent days count more) and tells you what will run out soon. It assumes a delivery takes about 7 days and adds a little extra just in case. The more sales you record, the more accurate it gets.

        Sample sales (for demos)

        The system can fill itself with about 6 months of random sample sales, stock purchases, returns and wastage so the dashboard, reports and forecast have something to show. Sample records are marked separately, so these buttons never touch real sales.

        Dashboard sections

        Choose which sections appear on the dashboard. The 4 summary cards at the top (Sales this month, Purchases, Gross profit, Items sold) are always shown. Changes save right away and apply to everyone in your shop.

        Staff discount limit

        The biggest discount a Staff account can give on one sale. The Owner can always give any discount.

        Peso-amount discounts count too: on a ₱1,000 sale with a 10% limit, Staff can give up to ₱100 off.

        Payment methods

        Turn off the ones your shop doesn't accept. They disappear from Checkout. Cash is always on.

        Auto log-out

        Signs out whoever is using this computer after a while with no clicks or typing, so no one can use an account that was left open.

        Applies to the Owner and all Staff. A warning shows 30 seconds before. Any unfinished sale in Checkout is cleared.

        Backup and restore

        Your shop's data is saved in this browser. If the browser data is cleared or the computer breaks, it's gone, so download a backup regularly and keep it on a flash drive or Google Drive.

        Last backup: never

        The backup has your products, sales, purchases, wastage, categories, shop settings and activity log. Accounts and passwords are not included, and a restore does not change them.
